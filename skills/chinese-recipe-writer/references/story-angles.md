# Story Angles for Recipe Articles

The Story section (~200-300 words EN) differentiates your content from competitors. Choose one angle per article.

## Angle 1: Historical Origin

Trace the dish's history. Include dates, places, people.

Example (Mapo Tofu):
```
Mapo Tofu was invented around 1862 in Chengdu by a woman named Chen Mapo (陈麻婆).
She ran a small restaurant near the Wanfu Bridge, serving workers and oil merchants.
Her face was marked with pockmarks (麻 má), which is how she got her nickname.
The dish she created — soft tofu in a spicy, numbing sauce — became so famous
that people traveled from across the province just to taste it...
```

## Angle 2: Personal/Family Story

First-person connection to the dish. Creates emotional resonance.

Example:
```
My grandmother made Mapo Tofu every Friday night. She said it was the dish that
reminded her of home — she grew up in a small village outside Chengdu...
```

## Angle 3: Cultural Significance

Explain the dish's role in Chinese culture, festivals, or daily life.

Example:
```
In Sichuan, Mapo Tofu isn't just a dish — it's a philosophy. The local saying goes:
"麻辣鲜香" (má là xiān xiāng) — numbing, spicy, fresh, fragrant — these four
flavors must be in perfect balance...
```

## Angle 4: Surprising Facts / Trivia

A lesser-known fact that creates curiosity.

Example:
```
Here's something most people don't know: Mapo Tofu was almost lost to history.
During the Cultural Revolution...
```

## Angle 5: Expectation vs Reality

Challenge what readers think they know (especially effective for international audiences).

Example:
```
If you've only had Mapo Tofu from your local Chinese takeout, I need to tell you
something: that's not real Mapo Tofu. The authentic version from Sichuan is an
entirely different experience...
```

## Tips for Story Writing

- For EN version: add more cultural context (foreign readers need background)
- For ZH version: can be more casual and assume shared knowledge
- For DE version: Germans appreciate precision — include specific dates and facts
- For JA version: Japanese readers know Chinese food well — focus on "authentic vs Japanese-style" differences
- For FR version: French readers appreciate culinary technique comparisons
